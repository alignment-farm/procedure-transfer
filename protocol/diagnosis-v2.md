# Acquisition diagnosis v2 — bounded development plan

14 September 2026; follows accepted comparison 5f636fb / publication 78965ca and updated brief 6960843. Original findings, protocol and evidence are immutable. This phase is diagnostic development, not a prospective transfer claim.

Primary hypotheses: (H1) reverse-KL weighting and resulting confident incorrect responses obstruct acquisition even with useful teacher predictions; (H2) the shared 0.0005 learning rate is unsuitable for soft-distribution training; (H3) teacher or token-prefix targets do not provide the intended task signal. Inspect the teacher on the actual training prefixes before interpreting optimizer outcomes. Finite derivatives alone are insufficient.

Use four new acquisition identifiers pelk, druvan, snebit, korvaz and four separate development-use identifiers halmek, przuna, veskolit, dunfepazor; all four conditions in each split. Retain original schema, tool mapping, uppercasing, suffix and generic induction reminder. No original final-evaluation cases inform tuning. Development-use cases and oracle answers may inform diagnostic selection; they are not final evidence for a transfer claim.

Initial matrix, seed 41, common initialization and shuffled input sequence: hard-label CE at 0.0005 (positive acquisition control); full reverse KL and full forward KL each at 0.0005 and 0.00005. Only orientation and rate vary in the 2x2 soft-target factorial. Same gold response prefixes and frozen example-conditioned teacher in all soft-target arms. 256 single-example steps (16 epochs), with diagnostics at steps 0,32,128,256; record training recall and development behavior separately, plus component correctness and policy probabilities. No early stopping or checkpoint replacement in this matrix. Canonical answer/EOS tokens and teacher targets identical across arms. Cache the 16 detached teacher distributions once; cache construction costs are separate and cannot be compared directly to the old uncached runtime.

Teacher audit: greedy acquisition correctness, canonical-target probabilities, decoded argmax tokens and tokenization agreement; contrast first-token probabilities and logit-space gradients under forward and reverse KL. Log finite real adapter gradients and post-training base/reset invariants. Save every evaluated checkpoint and response. Separate meaningful loss direction effects from learning-rate interactions. A regime with at least 15/16 final acquisition calls correct is a functioning acquisition candidate, not proof of transfer.

Follow-ups depend on outcomes, with a written amendment before each: isolate routing versus argument transformation if joint acquisition fails or copying remains weak; test a controlled rescue (such as changing gradient aggregation or acquisition warm start) if the first matrix leaves reverse-KL collapse unresolved; move a functioning regime to sampled prefixes if justified. New developed transfer claims require a new frozen selection rule and fresh evaluation after development. This phase can close on an explanatory intervention or demonstrated limitation; an unexplained negative matrix alone is not closure. Initial allocation: five small runs, 40 GB MLX peak, 45 minutes execution ceiling. No external model calls or new weight downloads anticipated.
